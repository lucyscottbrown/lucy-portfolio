Learn wine in five minutes a day

Merlo teaches you about wine in short daily lessons, so you feel confident choosing a bottle in a shop or from a restaurant wine list.

How it works

A little each day, and it stays with you

Short daily lessons

The main grapes, styles and regions, how to read a label, and how to match wine with food - one small step at a time.

Reviews at the right moment

Merlo brings back what you've learned just before you'd forget it, so it sticks rather than fading after a week.

A tasting journal

When you open a bottle, a two-minute guided tasting compares your notes with what's typical for that wine.

Learning, not drinking

You never need a glass to keep your streak

Every lesson and review works without wine. Tasting is there for when you happen to open a bottle, and it's never needed to keep your streak going.

Merlo is for people who enjoy wine but feel unsure in front of a wine list. It explains jargon when it uses it, and it never assumes you already know the answer.

Why a blackbird?

Named after the grape thief

Merlot is thought to be named after the blackbird (merle in French), which loved eating the ripe grapes. Merlo is Italian for blackbird - and Merlo is that bird.
